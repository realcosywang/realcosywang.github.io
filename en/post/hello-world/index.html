Hello World

  • Jan 31, 2026 08:34
  • 8-minute read

This is my first blog post

I started building this personal website mainly because the Year of the Horse Spring Festival is approaching, and my day job has entered its final stages, so I finally have time to explore something interesting—in other words, I’m free. But having this opportunity is good; I might as well take this chance to carve out my own little corner in the digital world. From now on, I’ll write whatever I want here, but it will definitely revolve around career development and personal interests. I’m not planning to write a diary here.

After the website was built, I kept hesitating about its meaning. I don’t know if this kind of thing is useful—maybe no one will ever visit it for hundreds of years, at most it might be slightly helpful when job hunting, and I don’t know if I can keep maintaining it. But as a friend said: it doesn’t need to be useful, just do it if you like it. It’s great if people notice your work, but enjoying the creative process is more important.

Looking back, I’ve been in the internet industry for almost 5 years now, and my journey has gone through different stages that happen to match the Dunning-Kruger effect theory.

Peak of Mount Ignorant

When I joined JD.com after graduating in June 2021, I had endless energy every day and would naturally wake up at 7 AM. I remember that summer in Chengdu, it rained almost every night, but no matter how it rained at night, by morning the skies would clear. The morning sun would evaporate the puddles on the road into the air, making it feel exceptionally comfortable. In this comfortable state, I’d hop on a shared bike and head to work, arriving at the company by around 8 AM. Back then, I could maintain high energy throughout the day until 9 PM without feeling tired. Compared to my current physical state, it was truly incredible.

A few months after joining, I felt I had everything under control. The tasks assigned to me were a piece of cake, so I was quite liked by my boss. But I wasn’t the only one the boss liked—there was another colleague too. At that time, I was ambitious and couldn’t allow myself to be caught up by others. Driven by competitiveness, I started secretly competing with that colleague, even feeling somewhat resentful toward them—when I was rewarded and they weren’t, I’d secretly rejoice; when they received honors but I didn’t, I’d feel indignant—just like concubines competing for favor in a palace drama.

After the 2022 Spring Festival, JD.com fired the first shot in China’s internet industry mass layoffs. We were working on an urgent major project at the time, working overtime day and night to catch up on progress. Suddenly one day, a colleague told me the team leader had called him in for a talk—he was being laid off. In the following days, I heard more and more news of colleagues being laid off. Colleagues who were fighting alongside us yesterday had to leave the company the next day. This had a huge impact on me, who had only been working for half a year—so we can be abandoned by the company at any time, so this industry is very unstable, so this is what textbooks call “the profit-seeking nature of capital.”

But ultimately, the layoff wave didn’t affect me. After one unsuccessful interview, I gave up the idea of job-hopping. My abilities were recognized by everyone, and my boss gave me more and more honors and rewards, which undoubtedly calmed my restless heart.

In April 2022, I was promoted for the first time with a 0% salary increase, compared to colleagues in other departments who basically got 10% raises—I was speechless. It would have been better not to give me the promotion. In October 2022, I was promoted for the second time. Two promotions in one year was unprecedented among my peers, but the salary increase was only 5%—I was speechless again. This was an insulting raise; two promotions combined were less than others’ single raise. I felt unfairly treated, and my heart became restless again.

Subsequently, the department’s development went from bad to worse—constant leadership changes, constant organizational changes, continuous sporadic layoffs, and rumors of the department being dissolved spread everywhere. All of this intensified my restless heart. I started preparing for interviews to jump ship, seeking a better and fairer platform.

In early 2024, I chose ByteDance from among Meituan, Alibaba, and ByteDance. In March, I was promoted for the third time, but by then I had already decided to resign. Ironically, on the day before I left, I received an email about a 10% salary increase.

Valley of Despair

After joining ByteDance, I was still full of confidence and anticipation for the new job, hoping to do well and get along with colleagues, because I believed I was still that energetic and hardworking person.

ByteDance uses Go, while JD.com used Java. Since I was switching tech stacks, I would spend extra time every night after overtime studying Go and work-related knowledge. But I couldn’t keep it up for long because work at ByteDance was completely different from JD.com. At JD.com, only during particularly busy times would one person work on multiple projects in parallel, but at ByteDance, everyone always had 3 concurrent tasks. Anyone who has studied computer science knows that single-core CPU time-slicing also consumes resources. Being unfamiliar with Go language while handling 3 parallel tasks left me physically and mentally exhausted (I later discovered the system architecture was also a mess). After working overtime and getting home at night, I had no energy left to study proactively.

Communication between colleagues also made me very uncomfortable. My desk was next to my mentor’s, but no matter big or small issues, he would only communicate with me via messages. Once when we were reviewing a document together, I had to schedule an online meeting to share the screen—we sat next to each other but had to wear headphones for the meeting. During lunch, although everyone sat together to eat, everyone else was holding their phone in one hand and chopsticks in the other, not talking to each other. As an ENFJ, I couldn’t stand this atmosphere of only work and interests, without any human connection.

Under these circumstances, I didn’t do my job well either. Due to slow progress, I caused a delivery delay, and we even had a special meeting to review and summarize. I began to doubt my own work abilities—when did I start being unable to do my job well? Unable to handle interpersonal relationships?

This job even made me depressed. At work, I had to endure the pain of a herniated disc. After work or on weekends, I just wanted to lie in bed aimlessly scrolling my phone—no more cycling, no more working out, no more cooking, no more interest in anything else. When someone realizes they’re depressed, their depression symptoms have actually been going on for a long time. So I made up my mind to leave this life. No matter how much money I earn, physical and mental health should always take precedence over everything. Work should and can only serve life. If the priorities are reversed, affecting health and time with family, then I should give up this job.

With mental pain plus the pain of a herniated disc, I decided not to stay in China’s internet environment. By coincidence, I saw a job opportunity at a foreign company in Dalian—where I went to school. After passing the interview, I sold my bicycle and resigned. By then, I had been in Chengdu alone for three years since graduation, made one or two close friends, left many wonderful memories, and had already considered Chengdu as my second hometown. I once thought I would settle down here, but in the end, I uprooted myself and transplanted to Dalian.

Slope of Enlightenment

The new job in Dalian is indeed much easier, truly achieving work-life balance. Lineman, this Thai company, is commendable in terms of business, technology, and work atmosphere (otherwise I wouldn’t have time to work on this blog). I’ve also made some good friends here.

However, small companies have their own issues. For example, middleware like MQ and Redis—at JD.com, you only needed to apply for specifications, but here you need to manually configure k8s Labels and node selectors. One wrong configuration means a production incident.

Paying attention to details isn’t a bad thing for me. Because my previous company completely packaged middleware as black boxes, I overlooked many technical details, knowing what but not why. Now, whenever I need to consider technical details myself, it’s an excellent opportunity to fill in the gaps. During my time at Lineman, I can feel continuous progress at both macro and micro levels technically.

By July this year, I will have been working for 5 years. Initially, at the Peak of Mount Ignorant, I was ambitious. Later, in the Valley of Despair, I suffered greatly. Now, I’m slowly moving forward on the Slope of Enlightenment. I remember writing a sentence in the acknowledgments of my master’s thesis:

As I depart, I hesitate about the road ahead, but fortunately someone has left consoling words: seek truth from above and below.

Now I no longer hesitate, but still firmly practice this saying: Seek truth from above and below.

Let’s move forward step by step toward the Plateau of Sustainability.
